RESEARCH METHODOLOGY & PHILOSOPHY

ABOUT THE EXPERIMENT

Investigating human perception, aesthetic discernment, and the psychological impact of creative authorship.

1. The Genesis of the Study

As generative diffusion models like Midjourney, Stable Diffusion, and Imagen achieve unprecedented textural fidelity, visual artifacts that previously betrayed artificial generation (such as distorted hands or inconsistent light vectors) have dramatically diminished.

This visual experiment was designed to ask a simple yet profound question: When creator identity is withheld, does an observer instinctively perceive human intentionality, or are our judgments fundamentally governed by visual polish and surface perfection?

2. Three Tiers of Complexity

The 15 subjects span three carefully calibrated complexity tiers:

• Beginner: Obvious textural distinctions, macro studio lighting discrepancies, and recognizable synthetic uniformity versus raw organic photography.

• Intermediate: Nuanced painterly glazes, complex architectural perspectives, and authentic natural wildlife photography with true environmental dust and optical bokeh.

• Advanced: State-of-the-art commercial photography simulations, fine glass internal caustics, and micro-textural denim weaves that test even trained digital artists and optical specialists.

3. Key Hypotheses & The "Effort Bias"

Psychological studies in aesthetic philosophy demonstrate an inherent "effort heuristic" — humans naturally attribute higher artistic and monetary value to objects they believe required physical labor, time, and patient mark-making.

By assessing which perceptual factors observers prioritize (Visual Quality, Effort, Authenticity, Originality, Style, or Familiarity), this experiment reveals whether aesthetic appreciation is intrinsic to the visual work or tethered to the perceived humanity of the maker.

4. Why This Matters

As synthetic imagery integrates seamlessly into journalism, commerce, social media, and fine art galleries, developing visual literacy is no longer merely an intellectual exercise — it is a foundational modern perceptual skill.
